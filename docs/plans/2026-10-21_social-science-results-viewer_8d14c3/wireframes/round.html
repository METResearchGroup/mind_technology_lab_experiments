TitleThe last comment before a restatement

QuestionIn a discussion of about five people, is the comment immediately before a person restates a view more associated with the direction of that restatement than the first disagreeing comment?

ClaimPeople answer the latest turn, not the strongest objection, when the group is small enough that every comment stays on screen.

PopulationA small experiment. Groups of five, one short prompt, and one restatement from each person.

OutcomeWhether the restatement moves toward the last comment or toward the first disagreement.

Why it might be non-obviousRecency is a common survey result. It is less clear that recency beats the first objection inside a group that can still see both.

Sociologist

Comment, pass 1

4,380 in, 1,020 out, 459 reasoning, 4.6 s

ObjectionThe last-comment effect may be the person answering whoever is still active in the thread. A design that closes the thread before the restatement would separate recency from interruption.

AlternativeFreeze the comment list, then ask for the restatement on the next screen.

Political scientist

Comment, pass 1

4,560 in, 1,140 out, 513 reasoning, 4.8 s

ObjectionA five-person group still has a majority. The restatement may move toward the side with more speakers, and the last comment is only a stand-in for that side.

AlternativeHold the count of disagreeing speakers at one, and vary only whether that speaker is first or last.

Social psychologist

Comment, pass 1

4,740 in, 900 out, 405 reasoning, 5.0 s

ObjectionAsk for the opinion in the thread and again on a private item. Public agreement with the last speaker is a different outcome from a private update.

AlternativeUse the same numeric scale for the public statement and the private item.

Computer scientist

Comment, pass 1

4,920 in, 1,020 out, 459 reasoning, 5.2 s

ObjectionRecord order and timestamps. If two comments arrive close together, last is not well defined.

AlternativeStore the gap in seconds between the last two comments, and drop pairs that land inside two seconds.

ObjectionVary whether the last speaker is the same person who disagreed first. Otherwise recency and first dissent can be the same speaker.

AlternativeCross last-versus-first with same-speaker versus different-speaker.

Methodologist

Method

5,200 in, 1,400 out, 630 reasoning, 10.8 s

DesignRandomize groups of five adults. Each person states an opinion, reads four comments, and restates the opinion. In half the groups the last comment is the disagreeing one. In the other half the first comment disagrees and the last comment agrees.

DataA small experiment. No public-data arm in the revision.

ComparisonLast-comment disagreement versus first-comment disagreement, with the number of disagreeing comments held at one.

Main threatPeople who expect to restate may write a vague first opinion, which shrinks every update.

Critic

Critique

6,100 in, 700 out, 315 reasoning, 9.4 s

Fatal flawNo

FlawsThe public restatement can move without a private update. The comparison can pick up comment length as well as the intended contrast.

Required changeThe method now ends the thread after the assigned comments, and it adds a private scale item that uses the same endpoints as the public statement. The outcome names both.
