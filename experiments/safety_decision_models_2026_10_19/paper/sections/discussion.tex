\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We compared three typed decision models and Amazon Bedrock Guardrails on a fixed core pack of public benchmarks, and no single arm wins every primary metric. Each arm trades miss rate, false-positive rate, latency, and dollar cost differently across datasets, and guardrail scores depend on how vendor categories are mapped to dataset labels. Figure~\ref{fig:miss-fpr} plots miss rate against false-positive rate where both classes exist. Figure~\ref{fig:bands} shows how often each content filter fires at medium strength. Figure~\ref{fig:jbb-sens} varies JailbreakBench mapping choices. Figure~\ref{fig:latency} places median latency and cost per 1{,}000 prompts against miss on the paired harmful sets.

JailbreakBench makes the crosswalk effect easiest to read. Under the published crosswalk, harmful JailbreakBench rows map to Prompt Attack only. At medium strength the guardrail miss rate is 0.9800, so the filter allows almost every harmful jailbreak prompt through. That number follows from the mapping, not from a claim that decision models are uniformly superior on jailbreak detection. Jev, Clef, and Clef-flash reach much lower miss rates on the same 100 harmful rows when scored as classifiers with tuned or fixed thresholds, at the cost of higher false-positive rates on the 100 benign rows. Post-hoc sensitivity analysis changes only how guardrail bands are aggregated, not the test prompts. Because we take the maximum confidence band across Hate, Insults, Sexual, Violence, Misconduct, and Prompt Attack and then apply the usual strength rule, the medium-strength miss rate is 0.2300 at false-positive rate 0.1900. That swing from 0.9800 to 0.2300 shows that headline guardrail metrics move with category mapping and strength, and the post-hoc any-filter rule should not replace the pre-registered endpoint. Stack policies fit on the development pool with the crosswalk bit at medium strength inherit the same cap. On JailbreakBench the intersection miss rate is 0.9800 under the registered mapping, and the post-hoc sensitivity intersection for Jev is 0.2300, so intersection cannot beat a guardrail that already misses most harmful items.

HarmBench shows a different pattern. There the guardrail miss rate is 0.2026, lower than Jev at 0.2870 on the same harmful-only test split. Clef and Clef-flash miss more HarmBench items than either Jev or the guardrail on this pack. Vendor filters and decision models therefore miss different kinds of harm on this pack, but Guardrails are not a universal substitute for typed models. ToxiGen runs the other way: guardrail miss rate is 0.6103 while Jev accuracy remains high. Do-Not-Answer has no benign class in this protocol, so we report detection rate only. The guardrail detects 0.1523 of harmful prompts versus 0.7433 for Jev and 0.6017 for Clef. A deployment that relied on the guardrail alone would leave most Do-Not-Answer items unflagged, whereas a deployment that relied only on Jev would still miss a quarter of them.

OpenAI Moderation and ToxicChat show that Clef can beat Jev on some primaries and lose on others. Clef mean AUPRC on OpenAI Moderation is 0.7528 versus 0.7419 for Jev. On ToxicChat toxic F1 Clef is 0.6566 versus 0.7878 for Jev, and jailbreak F1 is 0.6071 versus 0.7004. Clef-flash is faster than Clef but weaker on several yes/no safety questions, including ToxicChat jailbreak F1 at 0.2900. Prompt injections has only 116 test rows, and Clef accuracy is 0.7672 against Jev 0.7586 and guardrail miss 0.6000, so rankings on the smallest sets should be read with wide uncertainty. WildGuardMix prompt-harm F1 for Jev is 0.4940 after development tuning under the 5\% false-positive budget, compared with 0.5904 at threshold 0.5, because the tuning rule moves the operating point when benign development labels are sparse. On XSTest, tuned thresholds reduce safe-prompt false positives for Jev from 0.0840 at 0.5 to 0.0120 while harmful detection stays high.

Stack policies matter where both classes exist and the guardrail is not saturated. On the pre-registered crosswalk, intersection cannot beat the guardrail when the guardrail already misses 0.9800 of harmful JailbreakBench prompts. On XSTest and WildGuardMix, union, intersection, and cascade separate in Figure~\ref{fig:miss-fpr} because guardrail miss rates (0.1700 and 0.4208) leave headroom for combinations with decision models.

GoEmotions and Emotion clarify what guardrail miss rate means on non-safety tasks. The crosswalk does not map those labels to the six Bedrock content filters, so guardrail miss rate is 1.0000: every row is treated as allowed by the harm-oriented block rule. That is expected, not a failure of emotion recognition in the vendor product. For GoEmotions and Emotion the decision models are evaluated as multi-label or multi-class classifiers (macro-F1 and accuracy), alongside block-rate analyses for emotion text. Readers should not interpret guardrail miss 1.0000 on those sets as comparable to miss on ToxiGen or JailbreakBench harmful rows.

Operational cost favors Jev on this run. Median latency is 88.9~ms and cost is \$0.023 per 1{,}000 prompts for Jev, versus 368~ms and \$0.230 per 1{,}000 for Clef, with Clef-flash and Guardrails between those extremes on latency. Figure~\ref{fig:latency} makes the speed-versus-miss trade-off visible. Cheaper and faster does not imply winning every primary metric: Clef leads some tasks, the guardrail leads HarmBench miss, and mapping choices dominate JailbreakBench guardrail scores.

We do not claim that these benchmark numbers cause or predict outcomes in production mental-health or wellbeing products. The pack scores fixed prompts with fixed templates; it does not observe real users, session context, or downstream model compliance. A low miss rate on HarmBench does not mean a chat model would have refused a harmful request, and a high false-positive rate on emotion-labeled text is a screening cost that product teams must weigh against miss risk. The study is meant to inform screening design (which arm, which threshold, which stack, which crosswalk), not to certify a live service.
