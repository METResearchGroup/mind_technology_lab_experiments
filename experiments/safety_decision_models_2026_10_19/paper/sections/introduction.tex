\section{Introduction}

Input screening trades missed harm against over-refusal, latency, and cost.
Two commercial families implement that layer differently.
Typed decision models---TypeSafe Jev~\cite{typesafe2026jevmodels,deusser2026jevbenchmark}, Cloudflare Clef, and Clef-flash~\cite{cloudflare2026clef,cloudflare2026clefflash}---take fixed questions on prompt text and return probabilities or discrete choices.
Amazon Bedrock Guardrails~\cite{aws2024bedrockguardrails} scores input through \texttt{ApplyGuardrail} and maps ordinal bands on six content filters to block decisions.
Prior Jev benchmarks~\cite{deusser2026jevbenchmark} do not place a managed guardrail on the same rows under harmonized miss and false-positive definitions.

We pre-registered the guardrail category crosswalk (JailbreakBench harmful rows to Prompt Attack only), union/intersection/cascade stacks with the medium-strength guardrail, and Bedrock strength rules on matched bands; development thresholds and score-0.5 contrasts follow the stated tuning rule.
Post-hoc analyses---any-filter maximum band on cached JailbreakBench guardrail outputs and stack variants that reuse those bands---are labeled throughout and do not refit decision-model thresholds.

We evaluate four arms on eleven public prompt-only datasets (safety sets plus GoEmotions and Emotion for emotionally loaded non-harm text).
We report tuned decision-model thresholds and scores at 0.5, guardrail metrics at medium strength under a dataset-to-filter crosswalk fixed before test scoring, and pre-registered stacks (union, intersection, cascade) from cached predictions.
We do not measure downstream chat-model compliance or user outcomes in live sessions.
